# Customer Support Response Library

> **Fictional portfolio sample.** These templates use invented situations and placeholders. They are designed to show organization, tone, personalization, and escalation judgment; they are not messages sent to real customers.

## How to use this library

1. Select the template that matches the customer's main request.
2. Replace every item inside `[square brackets]` with verified information.
3. Remove lines that do not apply.
4. Check the account, order, policy, and promised date before sending.
5. Escalate any case involving fraud, safety, threats, legal requests, sensitive personal data, or an exception outside the approved policy.

## Voice guide

- Start by recognizing the specific reason the person contacted the team.
- Use short sentences and plain language.
- State what was checked, what happens next, and when the next update is due.
- Avoid blaming a customer, courier, teammate, or system.
- Do not promise a refund, replacement, credit, or deadline without authority.
- End with one clear next step rather than several vague offers.

## Escalation map

| Situation | Action | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safety, injury, or property damage | Stop standard troubleshooting and escalate immediately | Customer's exact words, product, order, time, and safe contact route |
| Suspected fraud or account takeover | Route to the security or billing queue | Case reference and verified account signals only |
| Threat, harassment, or self-harm language | Follow the organization's urgent-response policy | Exact language and time; avoid interpretation |
| Legal, regulator, or media request | Send to the authorized company contact | Sender, organization, request, deadline, and attachments |
| Sensitive personal information | Move to the approved secure channel | What was received, where it is stored, and who was notified |
| Policy exception | Ask the authorized owner for a decision | Standard rule, requested exception, evidence, and proposed deadline |

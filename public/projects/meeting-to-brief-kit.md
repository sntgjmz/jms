## Recap

The group discussed a short newsletter about creating a useful end-of-week desk routine. The proposed reader is someone working from a small home-office space. The content should be approachable, practical, and readable on a phone.

### Confirmed decisions within this exercise

- Use a three-step checklist as the main content.
- Write in a warm, direct voice with short paragraphs.
- Link the call to action to a plain-text checklist once its destination is approved.
- Require the brand owner's approval before any publication.

### Ideas requiring a decision

- Add a desk illustration or keep the email text-led.
- Make the checklist a separate download or include it in the email.
- Test a question-style subject line only if a testing process is available and approved.

## Content brief

**Working title:** A small reset for next week.

**Audience:** People who work at a compact home-office desk and want a simple weekly routine.

**Reader need:** A short, practical way to capture loose tasks and prepare for Monday.

**Main message:** A useful reset can be small: capture open loops, clear a little space, and choose one next step.

**Proposed format:** 250-350 words, one checklist, and one call to action. This is a planning target, not an established client requirement.

**Tone:** Warm, specific, and encouraging. Avoid productivity guarantees and unverified product claims.

**Outline:**

1. Open with the familiar feeling of ending the week with loose tasks.
2. Offer three steps: capture remaining tasks, return small items, choose Monday's first priority.
3. Invite the reader to adapt the routine to their own space.
4. Close with the approved checklist link.

**Sample subject lines for review:**

- A small reset for next week
- Three steps to close your workweek

**Sample opening:**

> Before you close your laptop, give next week a small head start. Capture the loose tasks, put a few things back in place, and choose one priority to return to on Monday.
